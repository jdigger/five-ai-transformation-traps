# The Pattern

<v-clicks>

- Budgets for AI transformation are at an all-time high
- Pilots are everywhere. Production impact is rare
- Most failures aren't technical — they're organizational
- The same five traps show up again and again, across industries

</v-clicks>

# Trap #1
## Chasing Pilots Instead of Problems

---
layout: two-cols
---

# Trap #1: Pilot-itis

The organization launches dozens of proof-of-concepts, but few ever reach production.

- "Let's try AI on X" — without a defined business outcome
- Success measured by demo quality, not by KPI movement
- No owner accountable for taking a pilot to scale
- Innovation theater: activity mistaken for progress

::right::

<v-click>

### The fix

- Start from a costed business problem, not a technology
- Define the production success metric **before** the pilot starts
- Every pilot gets a scale-or-kill decision date
- Fund the path to production, not just the experiment

</v-click>

# Trap #2
## Underestimating the Data & Process Debt

---
layout: two-cols
---

# Trap #2: Data & Process Debt

Teams assume the model is the hard part. It usually isn't.

- Fragmented, undocumented, low-trust data sources
- Business processes never designed to be machine-legible
- "Clean it later" becomes a permanent blocker
- Governance and access requests stall projects for months

::right::

<v-click>

### The fix

- Treat data readiness as a first-class deliverable, not overhead
- Fix the process before you automate it — automating a broken
  process just breaks things faster
- Stand up lightweight, reusable data/access pipelines early
- Make data owners part of the core team, not a request queue

</v-click>

# Trap #3
## Treating AI as an IT Project

---
layout: two-cols
---

# Trap #3: The IT-Project Trap

AI transformation gets routed to IT like it's an infrastructure rollout.

- No line-of-business ownership of outcomes
- Change management and training treated as an afterthought
- Success criteria defined in technical terms, not operational ones
- Frontline workers encounter the tool with no context or trust

::right::

<v-click>

### The fix

- Co-own every initiative with the business function it changes
- Redesign roles and workflows alongside the tooling
- Invest as much in adoption and training as in build
- Measure adoption and behavior change, not just deployment

</v-click>

# Trap #4
## Skipping the Trust & Risk Conversation

---
layout: two-cols
---

# Trap #4: The Trust Gap

Teams ship capability faster than they build the guardrails around it.

- No clear policy on what AI is/isn't allowed to decide
- Model errors and hallucinations discovered by end users, not by design
- Legal, security, and compliance looped in too late
- One visible failure erodes trust in every future initiative

::right::

<v-click>

### The fix

- Define risk tiers and human-in-the-loop checkpoints up front
- Build evaluation and monitoring into the initiative, not bolted on after
- Bring risk/legal/security in during design, not at launch review
- Be transparent with users about what the system can get wrong

</v-click>

# Trap #5
## Optimizing the Org Chart, Not the Capability

---
layout: two-cols
---

# Trap #5: The Org-Chart Trap

A "Head of AI" or a new committee gets created — and the org declares victory.

- Centralized team with no mandate or budget to actually ship
- Duplicate, competing efforts across business units
- Capability building (skills, platforms, reusable patterns) neglected
- Structure changes; ways of working don't

::right::

<v-click>

### The fix

- Build a durable platform + enablement layer, not just a title
- Give the AI function real authority: budget, roadmap, decision rights
- Invest in reusable capability — patterns, tooling, talent — not one-off wins
- Revisit structure only after the operating model is working

</v-click>

# The Common Thread

<v-clicks>

- All five traps are organizational, not technical
- They all trade the *appearance* of progress for real transformation
- The fix in every case: own the outcome, not the artifact

</v-clicks>
